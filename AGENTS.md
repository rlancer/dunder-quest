# Working in this repo

## Always open a PR

Finish every change by opening a pull request, without being asked: branch off `main`, commit, push, and `gh pr create`. CI deploys every PR to dev.dunderquest.com, which is where changes get reviewed, so a change that's only committed locally can't be seen yet. Never push straight to `main`: it deploys to production.

End with the PR link and a reminder that the preview will be on dev.dunderquest.com once CI finishes.

## Notes go here, not in hidden memory

Anything worth remembering between sessions (decisions, preferences, context) goes in this file, in a PR like any other change, so it can be seen and edited. Don't save it to an agent's private memory store.

## Layout

See [README.md](README.md). The site is built by `site/build.py`. Each game has its own art and trailer tools under `games/<game>/tools/`, run from that game's folder.

## Decisions

- **Generating assets is pre-approved.** Generate art, music and SFX with the `tools/generate_*.py` scripts (OpenRouter key in `.env`), or new scripts like them, without asking first. Generated beats placeholder. Keep trailer and marketing assets out of a game's `assets/`, so the web build stays small.
- **Dunder Quest is a Holy Grail homage.** Python is named after Monty Python. Use winking homage names (the Knight Who Says Mi, Tom the Enchanter, the Bridge of Peril), never the films' real character names, and don't quote at length in visible UI, to stay clear of Python (Monty) Pictures' IP. Hidden eggs (`common/eggs.gd`, listed at /admin/eggs) may quote short lines.
- **Flatmates.js is the second game** (`games/flatmates/`, its own Godot project, page at /flatmates/, game at /flatmates/play/). It teaches JavaScript and is a homage to a Croydon flat-share sitcom. Keep it distinct from Dunder Quest: no quiz duels; the puzzles are scripts that the flatmate you aren't playing runs, and left alone he drifts back to his comfort spot. Use homage names only (Clive, Jazz, Big Ron, Tess from Accounts), never the show's names or lines, and don't name the show in anything public. Its checks are `tests/bot.tscn` and `tests/verify_lessons.mjs`.
- **Curb Your Oxidation is the third game** (`games/curb/`, its own Godot project, page at /curb/, game at /curb/play/). It teaches Rust and is a homage to an LA cringe-comedy series: Rust's borrow checker is the unwritten social contract. Copy the show as closely as possible in everything that is format, tone or trope (the Westside setting, petty etiquette disputes, the improvised-feeling bickering, the hero's squinting stare-downs and suspicious interrogations, black title cards in heavy Franklin Gothic (the show's ITC Franklin Gothic Heavy is commercial, so use Libre Franklin, OFL, in `games/curb/assets/fonts/`, for the game, the site and the trailer), the show's yellow for the name and the accents on black (`Palette.YELLOW`, #fad41c), trailers with the music under every second and a fast cut of highlights (not the game played through; text cards held long enough to read), a freeze frame and a bouncy comic-tuba sting at the end of every scene), with exactly three limits: no real people's or characters' names (homage names only: Barry Danvers, Gil and Sharon Greene, Carol, Lamont, Ted Pruitt), no verbatim catchphrases or lines from the show, and no recreation of its theme tune (original music in the same style and instrumentation is fine). Don't name the show in anything public: the game, the site (brand kit included), the trailers and the video descriptions. The one exception is Rob's own social posts, which may say the game is "inspired by" the show by name, with nothing implying the network is involved. Their copy on the brand kit's launch page (`CURB_LAUNCH`) names it too, since it's what he pastes. Keep it distinct from the other two games: no quiz duels and no flatmate running your script; a plan plays in Barry's head first and Sharon interrupts at the line the borrow checker rejects, with the real rustc error code. Never teach anything that isn't real Rust. Its checks are `tests/cases.gd`, `tests/bot.tscn` and `tests/verify_rust.py` (which compiles every plan with rustc; CI runs it).
- **Mission Silicon is the fourth game, in prototype** (Episodes 8 to 13 playable; its checks are `tests/check.sh`: `cases.gd`, which makes each episode's data prove its lesson in the simulation, `coverage.gd`, `smoke.tscn` and `bot.tscn`) (`games/mission-silicon/`, page at /mission-silicon/, game at /mission-silicon/play/; not on the home splash yet). Pick it up from `games/mission-silicon/HANDOFF.md`. It teaches non-technical people enough about how web apps are built (languages and where they fit, frontend, backend, databases, caching, scaling, distributed systems) to build with AI; the player doesn't write code. Curriculum first, in `games/mission-silicon/CURRICULUM.md`; the game is designed from it in `games/mission-silicon/GAME.md` (the player reviews the AI's pull request, then a simulated live moment judges it; runway is the score). It's a homage to Silicon Valley, the HBO sitcom, its title turned around: the player is a non-technical founder launching an AI startup, Forkcast, the curriculum's running example, from an incubator house in the Mission, directing an overconfident AI coding assistant, Aleister Jr. How the show carries the curriculum (cast, episodes, format) is in `games/mission-silicon/SHOW.md`. The curriculum is the standing reference for which topics are covered; the show follows it, and every module must stay covered. Cast names wink at the show's the way Curb's do (Ulrich Bachner, Ricky Henley, Gilroy, Danesh Chaudhry, Gerard Dunlop, Wilson "Melon" Mellini, Galen Brightwater of Hubbub; full list in the handoff). The look is startup green (`#2BB673`) on off-white and slate, Montserrat ExtraBold title cards (OFL), original glitchy electronic music. It's built in Godot like the other games, landscape like them; keep the web build lean and phone-friendly, since this audience often arrives on a phone, with a "turn your phone sideways" card when held upright. Same limits as Curb: homage names only, no verbatim lines, no recreation of the theme tune, and don't name the show in anything players or viewers see (the game, the site, trailers, video descriptions). Unlike the other games, its planning docs in `games/mission-silicon/` do name the show and its characters, so the homage can be checked against the source. One exception to homage names: the title sequence, a San Francisco flyover parodying the show's intro, shows today's real AI companies by name (OpenAI, Anthropic, Nvidia...), as the show did. Companies only, never real people, drawn as pixel-art signs, not copies of their logo artwork.
- **The home page is a splash of every game, and Curb leads it.** Rob expects Curb Your Oxidation to be the hit, so it gets the big title card (trailer, Play button) at the top of `/`, and the bar's Play button goes to it; Dunder Quest and Flatmates.js follow as cards. Dunder Quest's own page is `/dunder-quest/` (`build_home`); its game stays at `/play/` and its lessons at `/lessons/`.
- **Trailers are YouTube-first.** The 4K master and the YouTube thumbnail are primary. Steam is deferred, so keep the 1080p master Steam-compatible.
- **One YouTube channel for every game:** [@dunderquest](https://www.youtube.com/@dunderquest). Channel art, copy and each video's upload form live on /brand/youtube/ (`build_brand_youtube` in `site/build.py`). Link that channel, not any other.

## Why it's a learning game

Rob is dyslexic and was failing his spelling tests until his dad wrote him a spelling game in BASIC; after that he got 100 on every one. His kids are 5 and 2 (as of October 2026), too young for Python, "but maybe some day". He offered this for launch copy. Use only these facts and don't embellish them (no made-up computer, age or names).
