# Working in this repo

## Always open a PR

Finish every change by opening a pull request, without being asked: branch off `main`, commit, push, and `gh pr create`. CI deploys every PR to dev.dunderquest.com, which is where changes get reviewed, so a change that's only committed locally can't be seen yet. Never push straight to `main`: it deploys to production.

End with the PR link and a reminder that the preview will be on dev.dunderquest.com once CI finishes.

## Notes go here, not in hidden memory

Anything worth remembering between sessions (decisions, preferences, context) goes in this file, in a PR like any other change, so it can be seen and edited. Don't save it to an agent's private memory store.

## Layout

See [README.md](README.md). The site is built by `site/build.ts`. Each game has its own art and trailer tools under `games/<game>/tools/`, run from that game's folder.

## Decisions

- **The site build and CI run on Bun, not Python.** `site/build.ts`, `scripts/fit_pages_limit.ts` and Curb's `tests/verify_rust.ts` are TypeScript run with `bun`, so CI needs no Python. Python stays only for the offline asset tools (`games/*/tools/*.py`, `site/curb_art.py`), which lean on Pillow and numpy and never run in CI. Flatmates.js's `verify_lessons.mjs` stays on `node`, since its page says every snippet is checked against Node.
- **Generating assets is pre-approved.** Generate art, music and SFX with the `tools/generate_*.py` scripts (OpenRouter key in `.env`), or new scripts like them, without asking first. Generated beats placeholder. Keep trailer and marketing assets out of a game's `assets/`, so the web build stays small.
- **Dunder Quest is a Holy Grail homage.** Python is named after Monty Python. Use winking homage names (the Knight Who Says Mi, Tom the Enchanter, the Bridge of Peril), never the films' real character names, and don't quote at length in visible UI, to stay clear of Python (Monty) Pictures' IP. Hidden eggs (`common/eggs.gd`, listed at /admin/eggs) may quote short lines.
- **Flatmates.js is the second game** (`games/flatmates/`, its own Godot project, page at /flatmates/, game at /flatmates/play/). It teaches JavaScript and is a homage to a Croydon flat-share sitcom. Keep it distinct from Dunder Quest: no quiz duels; the puzzles are scripts that the flatmate you aren't playing runs, and left alone he drifts back to his comfort spot. Use homage names only (Clive, Jazz, Big Ron, Tess from Accounts), never the show's names or lines, and don't name the show in anything public. Its checks are `tests/bot.tscn` and `tests/verify_lessons.mjs`.
- **Curb Your Oxidation is the third game** (`games/curb/`, its own Godot project, page at /curb/, game at /curb/play/). It teaches Rust and is a homage to an LA cringe-comedy series: Rust's borrow checker is the unwritten social contract. Copy the show as closely as possible in everything that is format, tone or trope (the Westside setting, petty etiquette disputes, the improvised-feeling bickering, the hero's squinting stare-downs and suspicious interrogations, black title cards in heavy Franklin Gothic (the show's ITC Franklin Gothic Heavy is commercial, so use Libre Franklin, OFL, in `games/curb/assets/fonts/`, for the game, the site and the trailer), the show's yellow for the name and the accents on black (`Palette.YELLOW`, #fad41c), trailers with the music under every second and a fast cut of highlights (not the game played through; text cards held long enough to read), a freeze frame and a bouncy comic-tuba sting at the end of every scene), with exactly three limits: no real people's or characters' names (homage names only: Barry Danvers, Gil and Sharon Greene, Carol, Lamont, Ted Pruitt), no verbatim catchphrases or lines from the show, and no recreation of its theme tune (original music in the same style and instrumentation is fine). Don't name the show in anything public: the game, the site (brand kit included), the trailers and the video descriptions. The one exception is Rob's own social posts, which may say the game is "inspired by" the show by name, with nothing implying the network is involved. Their copy on the brand kit's launch page (`CURB_LAUNCH`) names it too, since it's what he pastes. Keep it distinct from the other two games: no quiz duels and no flatmate running your script; a plan plays in Barry's head first and Sharon interrupts at the line the borrow checker rejects, with the real rustc error code. Never teach anything that isn't real Rust. Its checks are `tests/cases.gd`, `tests/bot.tscn` and `tests/verify_rust.ts` (which compiles every plan with rustc; CI runs it).
- **Mission Silicon is the fourth game, in prototype** (`games/mission-silicon/`, page at /mission-silicon/, game at /mission-silicon/play/; a card on the home splash). Only the Pilot is playable while the season is rebuilt; it's a web app in `games/mission-silicon/web/` (React + Vite + TypeScript, bun), and its checks are `bun run typecheck && bun run test` there. Pick it up from `games/mission-silicon/HANDOFF.md`. It teaches non-technical people enough about how web apps are built (languages and where they fit, frontend, backend, databases, caching, scaling, distributed systems) to build with AI; the player doesn't write code. Curriculum first, in `games/mission-silicon/CURRICULUM.md`; the game is designed from it in `games/mission-silicon/GAME.md` (the player reviews the AI's pull request, then a simulated live moment judges it; runway is the score). It's a homage to Silicon Valley, the HBO sitcom, its title turned around: the player is a non-technical founder launching an AI startup, Forkcast, the curriculum's running example, from an incubator house in the Mission, directing an overconfident AI coding assistant, Aleister Jr. How the show carries the curriculum (cast, episodes, format) is in `games/mission-silicon/SHOW.md`. The curriculum is the standing reference for which topics are covered; the show follows it, and every module must stay covered. Cast names wink at the show's the way Curb's do (Ulrich Bachner, Ricky Henley, Gilroy, Danesh Chaudhry, Gerard Dunlop, Wilson "Melon" Mellini, Galen Brightwater of Hubbub; full list in the handoff). The look is startup green (`#2BB673`) on off-white and slate, Montserrat ExtraBold title cards (OFL), original glitchy electronic music. Unlike the other games it's a web app, not Godot (Rob, November 2026: it had become a learning platform more than a traditional game, and the web reads better and loads faster on a phone); it works held either way. The story is pixel art (backdrops, portraits); the lessons are clean, readable cards in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. The cast talk the player through it: lines are voiced with `web/tools/voice.ts`, always with the words on screen and a mute button. Keep the first download small, since this audience often arrives on a phone. Same limits as Curb: homage names only, no verbatim lines, no recreation of the theme tune, and don't name the show in anything players or viewers see (the game, the site, trailers, video descriptions). Unlike the other games, its planning docs in `games/mission-silicon/` do name the show and its characters, so the homage can be checked against the source. One exception to homage names: the title sequence, a San Francisco flyover parodying the show's intro, shows today's real AI companies by name (OpenAI, Anthropic, Nvidia...), as the show did. Companies only, never real people, drawn as pixel-art signs, not copies of their logo artwork.
- **Mission Silicon is being reworked to teach first** (Rob, November 2026). The prototype's episodes didn't teach and were hard to follow, so every episode is up for redesign; nothing in the current season has to be kept. Make it fun, but the point is that players actually learn. The template is the Level One storyboard (`games/mission-silicon/storyboard/pilot.html`): the map first, then each episode built around a few real architecture decisions, each shown side by side against the episode's requirements as the player picks (yes, partly or no, with the reason), then reading the AI's plan, a live moment whose results trace to a cause, and a decision record. Real product names (Next.js, Supabase, Swift...) appear as the real term under each plain description, never as advice to buy one. A choice that fails the episode still says when it would be the right one. Storyboard each episode before building it. Rob, November 2026, after playing the web Pilot: it felt like corporate training ("tap each one"), so episodes play as an interactive sitcom instead: the cast walk about a pixel-art set as full-body sprites (`tools/sprites.py`) and argue the trade-offs in character, the whiteboard fills in as they argue, and the player makes the call; a wrong pick plays a flash-forward of it going wrong, then its champion says when it would be right. No tap-each-card screens. More generated art is fine (the image model is cheap).
- **Mission Silicon has a fourth attempt that is a different game, the Human Backend** (`games/mission-silicon/human/`, at /mission-silicon/human/; November 2026, after Rob said none of the storyboard, the sitcom Pilot or the Lab was fun and asked for something original). The AI built the app and forgot the backend, so the player is the backend: requests land on the desk as paper slips and get dragged to the thing that can answer them (sticky notes are memory, Gerard's book is the database, the stamp trusts the phone, the adding machine checks, the Model costs a dollar, the key is the API key). Its one rule: Aleister Jr. learns each job by watching it done three times and then does it that way at speed, so a shortcut comes back automated. Nothing is marked right or wrong when it's done; the night decides, and real terms are stamped on things only after the night that showed what they are. No cards to read, no choosing from a list. Vanilla TypeScript + Vite (no React), sound effects synthesised in code, art from `human/tools/art.py`. Why this concept and not four others is in `games/mission-silicon/HUMAN_BACKEND.md`. Its checks are `bun run typecheck && bun run test` there (`engine.test.ts` proves each night's lesson with bot players; `ui.test.ts` plays the real screen). Whether it replaces the Pilot at /mission-silicon/play/ is Rob's call after playing it.
- **Mission Silicon has three sketches of how to teach it, Three Ways In** (`games/mission-silicon/ways/`, at /mission-silicon/ways/; November 2026, after Rob asked to explore three different ways to explain how to get the skills to vibe code). They are sketches for comparing, not a fifth game: each teaches the same three things (where data lives, who decides the price, where the secret key goes) so the ways can be told apart. One Tap is watching (one picture of the app, a switch, the same tap sent through both ways). The Gaps is saying (the player types the brief in their own words, Aleister Jr. builds that and guesses the rest, Thursday night finds the guesses, and every fix is a sentence). Field Trip is doing it for real (three errands to run with whatever AI the player already uses; the page marks nothing, the player reports what they saw and it says what that means). Vanilla TypeScript + Vite like the Human Backend; checks are `bun run typecheck && bun run test` there. Which way, if any, the game is rebuilt around is Rob's call after trying them.
- **The home page is a splash of every game, and Curb leads it.** Rob expects Curb Your Oxidation to be the hit, so it gets the big title card (trailer, Play button) at the top of `/`, and the bar's Play button goes to it; Dunder Quest, Flatmates.js and Mission Silicon follow as cards. Dunder Quest's own page is `/dunder-quest/` (`buildHome`); its game stays at `/play/` and its lessons at `/lessons/`.
- **Trailers are YouTube-first.** The 4K master and the YouTube thumbnail are primary. Steam is deferred, so keep the 1080p master Steam-compatible.
- **One YouTube channel for every game:** [@dunderquest](https://www.youtube.com/@dunderquest). Channel art, copy and each video's upload form live on /brand/youtube/ (`buildBrandYoutube` in `site/build.ts`). Link that channel, not any other.

## Why it's a learning game

Rob is dyslexic and was failing his spelling tests until his dad wrote him a spelling game in BASIC; after that he got 100 on every one. His kids are 5 and 2 (as of October 2026), too young for Python, "but maybe some day". He offered this for launch copy. Use only these facts and don't embellish them (no made-up computer, age or names).
