# Mission Silicon

The fourth Dunder Quest game, being built. It teaches non-technical founders enough about how web apps are built to direct an AI that builds them. The design is in [GAME.md](GAME.md), the curriculum in [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md), the homage in [SHOW.md](SHOW.md), and where it stands in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

**Now a web app, being rebuilt to teach first.** What `/mission-silicon/play/` serves is [web/](web/): the Pilot, voiced, in React + Vite + TypeScript (built with bun; `bun run dev` to play it locally, `bun run test` for its checks, `bun tools/voice.ts` to record new lines). Its words are `data/pilot.json`. The rest of this README describes the Godot prototype, kept for reference while the season is rebuilt; it is no longer exported or checked in CI. See [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

**The Godot prototype: Episodes 1, *Pilot*, 2, *The Whiteboard*, 3, *Tabs and Spaces*, 4, *Demo Day*, 5, *The Spreadsheet*, 6, *Log In*, 7, *Launch Party*, 8, *Viral*, 9, *The Board Meeting*, 10, *Hubbub Dine*, 11, *Charged Twice*, 12, *The Leak*, 13, *The Conference*, and 14, the *Finale*: the whole season, playable end to end.** In the Pilot, rebuilt teaching-first from its storyboard, you learn the map of a web app (the diner's device, our server, the database, the AI model), follow one search through it, and make four decisions against Thursday's requirements before Aleister writes a line: web app, installable web app, native or cross-platform; what goes on the phone, the server or the database; build the backend or rent it; and reading the tech stack to catch a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` key, before Walter's 200 testers try it by device and an iPhone bug at 4:40 shows how fast a web fix lands; in Episode 2 the waitlist page has to survive Ulrich's party and his live edit to the headline, with or without Git to roll it back. In Episode 3 the team argues about which language to write the backend in, and Friday's beta finds out what types catch, what they don't, and what happens when the backend is a notebook on someone's laptop; in Episode 4 the investors try Forkcast on their phones at Demo Day, one of them edits the booking fee in his browser, and a bot reads the AI key out of the app. In Episode 5 the bookings live in Gerard's spreadsheet until two diners book at once, and then the AI deletes every restaurant, so it matters whether anyone ever restored a backup; in Episode 6 Forkcast gets logins, bots try passwords leaked from another site, a restaurateur changes a number in the address bar, and it matters who keeps the passwords and how. In Episode 7 Forkcast launches at Ulrich's party, deployed from Melon's laptop unless you ask how the code gets there, and Ulrich ships three changes live: tests that only check the home page opens let a broken booking through, staging catches a setting the host doesn't have, and only error tracking and a rollback save the change nobody could have caught. From Episode 8, Aleister Jr. answers a celebrity chef's post with a pull request; you keep or cut its changes, ask it questions, set the dials, and merge. Then Friday night runs: diners pour through what you built, the garage server overheats (or doesn't), and Gerard's postmortem says what went wrong, in the real terms. Fail, and it's take two from the same board. In Episode 9 the board installs an adult CEO who wants a dashboard on every screen and a box to sell, and Friday's bookings have to survive the board watching the numbers live. In Episode 10 the tech giant launches a copy of Forkcast in the dinner rush, and its scraper hammers your API while its two hundred microservices fall over on stage. In Episode 11 the restaurants want deposits: the payment provider times out on a busy Friday, retries charge diners twice unless every payment carries a key, two parties race for the last table, and Ricky's network of diners' phones can't agree on anything. In Episode 12 Melon has put the AI key in a public repository and Hubbub sues: deleting the file doesn't stop strangers running up the bill, the package the AI suggests doesn't exist until someone malicious registers it, and a bot reads other diners' bookings by counting through the numbers in the links. In Episode 13 the concierge has to book a table by itself, live on a conference stage: without the real menus it makes dishes up, and a review planted for AIs tries to steer it, so how much it can do and what it must ask first decide how bad that gets. In the Finale the AI proposes every box it knows at once for sixty cities, and the night replays the whole season (the surge, the dashboards, the scraper, the deposits, the leaked key, the planted review), so you cut it back to the simplest version that works, and the season ends ten years later.

The prototype is its own Godot 4.7 project, beside `../curb/` and the others; `tests/check.sh` still runs its checks locally. The web app is built to `/mission-silicon/play/` by `scripts/export_web.sh`, beside the game's page at `/mission-silicon/` (`build_mission_silicon` in `site/build.py`). It's a card on the home page's splash, after Dunder Quest and Flatmates.js.

## How it's built

- `data/pieces.json`: the catalogue of pieces (app servers, the garage server, Postgres, Redis, CDN, queue, model API...) with their capacities, work per kind of request, latencies and costs. Illustrative numbers; the shapes are real.
- `data/season.json`: the season outline (every episode and its curriculum modules) and the built episodes: cast, cold open, brief, budget, starting board, the pull request's cards, the asks, the dials, the live moment (traffic, events, the hotfix, the cast's reactions), the goal, the solution, the postmortem findings, the codex cards and the cliffhanger.
- `sim/plan.gd`: what the player decided (cards kept, questions asked, dials, hotfix answers), turned into the board the simulation runs.
- `sim/sim.gd`: the live moment as a deterministic flow model. Each tick is a simulated minute: queueing below capacity and a backlog above it, the garage server overheating, cache hit rates (x / (1 + x), x = rate × TTL / keys) and staleness, shared caches leaking per-user data, logouts from servers that aren't stateless, Postgres's connection limit, the model provider's rate limit, a scraper against our own per-client rate limit, a chain of calls between microservices, payment calls that time out after the money moved, retries with and without idempotency keys, the race for the last table (quadratic in the traffic) and the transaction that closes it, a leaked key used by strangers until it's rotated (from the Git history, the app's own code or a malicious package), a bot reading bookings without an ownership check, an agent's model calls and tools, answers made up without retrieval, a planted review steering a share of answers (cut sharply by marking what the model reads as data, never to none), and what a steered agent can do with or without a confirmation step and its admin tools, the bill.
- `data/pilot.json` and `scenes/pilot.gd`: Episode 1, the Pilot, rebuilt teaching-first from [storyboard/pilot.html](storyboard/pilot.html) as its own scene (the map, one search, four decision boards checked against Thursday's requirements, Thursday by device, the decision record). Its rules (which options fit, the 200 seeded testers, the marks) are in `core/pilot_model.gd`, which `cases.gd` checks; the season screen's first tile opens it. The old Pilot's pull-request data is gone from `season.json`.
- `scenes/episode.gd`: one script builds any episode from the data. Episodes 2 to 14 still play this way. `ui/board.gd` draws the whiteboard and the live traffic, `ui/pr_panel.gd` the pull request, card detail and dials, `ui/dialogue.gd` and `ui/card.gd` the dialogue, title cards, codex and cut to black, `ui/sideways.gd` the "turn your phone sideways" card.
- Laid out at 480×270 like Curb, taps only, every button at least 30×30 and no text under 9 pixels (about 44 and 13 points on a phone held sideways).
- Art: the cast's close-ups (`assets/portraits/`, 48×48) and the backdrops (`assets/backgrounds/`, 480×270: the garage, the kitchen, the boardroom, the keynote stage, the trattoria's host stand, the law firm's conference room, the conference's main stage, the house's living room, the documentary's interview chair, the house at dusk behind the title) are generated by `tools/art.py` with the Dunder Quest scenery helpers, then pixelated and cut to a small palette. Every face is an archetype from its `cast` entry in `data/season.json`, never a real person. `ui/portrait.gd` and `ui/backdrop.gd` just draw them.
- Sound: original glitchy electronic music (bitcrushed synths, a drum machine) for the title, planning and the live night, and stings for the title card, the merge, a failed night and the postmortem, in `assets/audio/`. `tools/music.py` generates them with the Dunder Quest music helpers, loops the music with `make_loops.py`, cuts each sting to a few seconds and encodes everything as mono Vorbis at quality 1, below the other games' setting, to keep the phone build small. The `Sound` autoload (`core/sound.gd`) plays them; on the web it waits for the first tap, and it stays quiet headless.
- The web pack is 1.9 MB (153 KB before the art and sound), about 1.5 MB of it music.
- The title cards are Montserrat ExtraBold (`assets/fonts/Montserrat.ttf`, OFL), trimmed to Latin characters with fonttools' `pyftsubset` to 31 KB; trim any new copy the same way.

## Checks

```sh
python tools/art.py                                          # the close-ups and backdrops that are missing (OpenRouter key in the repo's .env)
python tools/flyover.py                                      # the title flyover's parallax layers that are missing
python tools/music.py                                        # the music and stings that are missing
godot --headless --path . --import
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/coverage.gd   # COVERAGE result=ok: every curriculum module has an episode
godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/cases.gd      # CASES result=ok: each episode's data proves its lesson
godot --headless --path . res://tests/smoke.tscn             # SMOKE result=ok: everything loads, every music track and sting included
godot --headless --path . res://tests/bot.tscn               # BOT result=ok: plays every episode through the UI, take one and take two
godot --path . res://tests/bot.tscn -- shots                 # the same, with screenshots into tests/shots/ (needs a window)
godot --path . res://tests/bot.tscn -- shots phone=844x390   # at a phone held sideways, also checking sizes on its screen
bash tests/check.sh                                          # all of it
godot --path . res://tools/site_art.tscn                     # after the shots: the page's screenshots and social card, into site/static/img/
```

`cases.gd` runs the simulation headless for every episode: the solution passes and makes par; Aleister's pull request as proposed fails, and so does doing nothing; flipping any one decision fails the night, costs runway or is marked `free`, and every failure's top finding has a postmortem entry; Ulrich's card matters; and a seeded search (every card combination, every pair of dial changes, 3,000 random plans) finds nothing that passes with more runway than the solution. An episode with a `capstone` list (the Finale) also removes each part the season added to the board, one at a time, and the solution's night must fail every time with a finding. The bot checks the nights it plays on screen match the headless runs exactly.
